# Turkish pronunciation (practical summary)

*Updated 2026-11-03 · OpenAI (draft), reviewed and corrected*

Turkish is highly phonemic: most letters map to one sound, and every letter is pronounced.

First priorities: the eight vowels (a e ı i o ö u ü), vowel harmony, and the consonant letters that differ from English. Learn ı as a back, unrounded vowel (not /i/, not schwa) and ö/ü as front vowels with rounded lips. There are no diphthongs: pronounce adjacent vowels separately, and do not reduce unstressed vowels.

Vowel harmony: suffixes copy the vowel type of the word. Front vowels (e i ö ü) take front-vowel suffixes, back vowels (a ı o u) take back-vowel suffixes; rounding also matters (ev-ler, at-lar).

Stress is usually on the last syllable, with common exceptions (place names, some suffixes, many loanwords): copy native stress instead of English stress.

Consonants: c = /dʒ/, ç = /tʃ/, ş = /ʃ/, j = /ʒ/. ğ is never a hard g: it lengthens the vowel before it. r is a short tap. k, g and l next to front vowels are pronounced further forward (palatalised).

Final consonants: native words are spelled with final p, t, ç, k, and the sound switches to b, d, c, ğ before a vowel suffix (kitap → kitabı, kanat → kanadı, ağaç → ağacı, çocuk → çocuğu).

## Sounds

| Letter | Name | How | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ı** | dotless i | Close back unrounded vowel: tongue pulled back, lips spread. Not English /i/ and not a schwa. | ılık |
| **ö** | front rounded o | Mid front vowel with rounded lips, between French 'peu' and 'peur'. No English equivalent. | göz |
| **ü** | front rounded u | Close front vowel with rounded lips, like French 'u': say /i/ and round your lips. | gül |
| **ğ** | soft g | Not a stop. It lengthens the preceding vowel; between front vowels it may be a light y. | dağ |
| **c** | ce | Voiced affricate /dʒ/, like English j in 'jam'. | cam |
| **ç** | çe | Voiceless affricate /tʃ/, like English ch in 'chair'. | çay |
| **ş** | şe | Voiceless fricative /ʃ/, like English sh in 'ship'. | şeker |
| **r** | re | Short alveolar tap [ɾ], like the tt in American 'butter' or a single Spanish r. | ara |
| **k g l** | palatalised | Before and after front vowels (e i ö ü) they are pronounced further forward and 'lighter'; next to back vowels they are normal. | kitap, gül, el |

## Common mistakes

- Replacing ı with /i/ or a schwa (ılık as 'ilik').
- Pronouncing ö and ü without lip rounding (göz as 'gez', gül as 'gil').
- Ignoring vowel harmony when adding suffixes.
- Reading ğ as a hard g.
- Using English stress and reducing unstressed vowels to schwa.
- Reading c as /k/ or /s/ instead of /dʒ/.
- Using an English r instead of a short tap.
- Pronouncing the final p, t, ç, k with a long English puff, or keeping the final sound when a suffix follows (kitabı, not kitapı).

## Where to listen

⭐ = recommended

- ⭐ [Forvo: Turkish](https://forvo.com/languages/tr/) · *native-speaker, free to listen* — Short native recordings of single words, good for checking ı, ö, ü, ğ and stress.
- ⭐ [TurkishClass101](https://www.turkishclass101.com/) · *course, free and paid* — Graded lessons with slow, clear native speech and sentence-level practice.
- ⭐ [YouGlish: Turkish](https://youglish.com/turkish) · *native-speaker, free to listen* — Shows a word spoken in real videos by many native speakers, so you hear it in sentences.
- [Tureng Turkish-English Dictionary](https://tureng.com/en/turkish-english) · *dictionary, free to listen* — Good for meanings and examples; audio is not available for every entry, so check it before relying on it.
- [Google Translate Turkish](https://translate.google.com/?sl=tr&tl=en) · *tts, free to listen* — Quick check only; synthetic prosody is less reliable than native recordings.
